# South African Insurance Customer Retention — Key Findings

Eight SQL analyses of a fictional South African short-term insurance dataset.

## Finding 1: Customer Policy Ownership

### Business Insight

All 320 customers have at least one policy recorded. Of these, 180 (56%) hold one policy and 140 (44%) hold multiple policies.

This suggests potential cross-selling opportunities among single-policy customers.

However, the figures include cancelled policies, so we still need to determine how many customers currently have active insurance cover.

### Strategic Recommendation

The insurer should investigate which customers have active policies, the types of insurance they hold and why some policies were cancelled.

Customers without active cover should be prioritised for retention efforts.

Customers with one active policy could be considered for additional insurance products based on their needs and existing cover.

## Finding 2: Insurance Policy Cancellations

### Business Insight

Of 460 insurance policies, 71 (15.4%) have been cancelled.

Contents insurance has the highest cancellation rate (17.2%), followed by Personal Accident (15.9%), Home (15.7%) and Vehicle (14.1%).

The rates are fairly similar across products, suggesting that cancellations may be linked to wider issues such as pricing or customer service.

Vehicle insurance has the most cancellations (26), despite having the lowest cancellation rate.

### Strategic Recommendation

The insurer should investigate Contents insurance because it has the highest cancellation rate, followed by Vehicle insurance because it has the most cancelled policies.

Management should investigate premium increases, claims experiences, missed payments and whether customers still hold other active policies.

Understanding why customers cancel will help the insurer improve retention and avoid offering unsuitable products.

## Finding 3: Premium Increases and Policy Cancellations

### Business Insight

398 of the 460 policies (87%) experienced premium increases.

These policies had a cancellation rate of 16.6%, compared with 9.3% for policies without increases.

Of the 71 cancelled policies, 66 had experienced premium increases.

This suggests that rising premiums may contribute to cancellations, although we haven't confirmed whether the increases happened before customers cancelled.

### Strategic Recommendation

The insurer should investigate whether larger premium increases lead to higher cancellation rates and whether customers cancel soon after an increase.

Management should also compare policy types, policy age and claims history to understand what else may influence cancellations.

The insurer could improve renewal communication, review large premium increases and contact customers who may be at risk of cancelling. Any pricing changes should remain fair and compliant.

## Finding 4: Claims Experience and Policy Cancellations

### Business Insight

Policies with rejected claims had the highest cancellation rate (31.4%), compared with 14.6% for policies without claims and 12.5% for policies with other claims.

This suggests that rejected claims may contribute to customers cancelling their insurance, although further investigation is needed.

Only 35 policies had rejected claims, accounting for 11 of the 71 cancellations.

### Strategic Recommendation

The insurer should investigate why claims were rejected and whether customers cancelled shortly afterwards.

Management should review claim processing times, communication and the reasons customers gave for cancelling.

Customers who experienced both rejected claims and premium increases should receive particular attention.

Clearer explanations, easier claim reviews and better communication could help improve customer retention.

## Finding 5: Premium Payments and Policy Cancellations

### Business Insight

273 of the 460 policies (59%) had at least one missed premium payment.

These policies had a cancellation rate of 18.3%, compared with 11.2% for policies without missed payments.

Of the 71 cancelled policies, 50 had missed payments. However, 223 policies with missed payments remain active.

This suggests that missed payments may be an important warning sign for customer retention.

### Strategic Recommendation

The insurer should investigate why customers miss payments and whether missed payments happen before cancellations.

Management should introduce early payment reminders, review failed debit orders and consider adjusting payment dates to match customer paydays.

Customers who repeatedly miss payments could receive additional support before their policies are cancelled.

The insurer should also investigate customers who experience missed payments alongside premium increases or rejected claims.

## Finding 6: Customer-Level Retention Risk

### Business Insight

Of 460 insurance policies, 71 (15%) have been cancelled. However, only 29 of the 320 customers (9%) have cancelled all their policies.

Another 37 customers have cancelled one policy but still hold active cover. Together, they account for 37 cancelled policies.

Overall, 291 customers (91%) still have at least one active policy.

This shows why the insurer should measure customer retention separately from policy cancellations.

### Strategic Recommendation

The insurer should prioritise contacting the 37 partially cancelled customers to understand why they dropped a policy and protect their remaining cover.

The 29 fully cancelled customers should receive targeted win-back offers based on their reasons for leaving.

Management should also investigate premium increases, missed payments and rejected claims before deciding how to engage these customers.

Customers with active policies could be considered for additional suitable cover, provided they do not already hold it.

## Finding 7: Combined Customer Retention Risks

### Business Insight

Policies with more warning signs have higher cancellation rates.

Policies with no warning signs had a cancellation rate of 3.1%, compared with 10.2% for one sign, 19.9% for two and 25% for three.

Of the 71 cancelled policies, 53 (75%) had two or more warning signs.

However, most policies with multiple warning signs remain active, and we haven't established whether these signs occurred before cancellation.

### Strategic Recommendation

The insurer should prioritise investigating active policies with two or more warning signs, particularly the 12 active policies showing all three.

Management should examine which combinations of warning signs are most closely associated with cancellations, whether the signals occur early enough for intervention, and the reasons customers cancel.

The insurer could then test a targeted retention programme before introducing an automated alert system.

## Finding 8: Customer Retention by Segment

### Business Insight

Mass Market has the highest policy cancellation rate (17.4%), accounting for 39 cancelled policies and 18 of the 29 customers who lost all their insurance cover.

Affluent follows with a 15.6% cancellation rate and six fully cancelled customers. Entry has a 13.4% cancellation rate, while Premium has the lowest at 4.8%.

Mass Market presents the largest retention challenge because it accounts for the most cancelled policies and customers who have left entirely.

### Strategic Recommendation

The insurer should prioritise investigating cancellations among Mass Market customers, followed by Affluent customers.

Management should compare cancellation reasons, premium income, policy types, payment behaviour and claims experiences across segments.

Retention strategies should address the problems identified within each segment, rather than offering the same solution to every customer.

The insurer should test targeted interventions before introducing them across its customer base.
